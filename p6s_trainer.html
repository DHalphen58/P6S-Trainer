Reading convention: UPPERCASE (U D L R C) = front face. lowercase (u d l r c), used in the case definitions, = back face labeled after an x2 flip. The diagram below reflects that the scramble ends on a y2: the puzzle is left holding what was originally the back face toward the solver, so that's shown as "Front," and the original front (now facing away) is shown as "Back." This may look mirrored left-right relative to how the lowercase case labels are defined (x2) — that's expected, both are correct, just different viewing conventions for the same physical dials.

Still worth spot-checking: the back-linkage co-rotation sign is derived from first principles, not a verified source. Run one scramble on a real clock and tell me if the back face is off.
